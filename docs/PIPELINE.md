# slashyear — rebuild with verifiable claims

The site slashyear.com was a directory of 809 year pages whose content was written in an
encyclopedic voice but carried no sources at all: no citations, no references, no about
page. Nothing on it could be checked, which for a history reference is the whole
ballgame.

This rebuild keeps the site identical to look at and changes where the words come from.
Every sentence on every page is lifted from a specific revision of a specific Wikipedia
article, cleaned mechanically, and published with a link back to that exact revision.
Nothing is written by us and nothing is generated by a language model, so there is no
sentence on the site that a reader cannot check against its source in one click.

## What is guaranteed, and how

`pipeline/verify.py` walks the chain for every published bullet:

1. the published sentence is re-derived from the stored wikitext line and must match
   character for character;
2. that wikitext line must be present verbatim in the cited article revision;
3. with `--live`, the stored revision is re-downloaded from Wikipedia and compared byte
   for byte against the local copy.

Last full run: **86,902 bullets, 0 failures**, across 3,078 year pages, with all 3,078
cited revisions re-downloaded and confirmed byte-identical. The report is written to
`verification.json`. A revision the parallel sweep cannot reach is retried serially
rather than counted, because a throttled fetch is no answer and not a mismatch.

Step 0 is a source check the other three cannot make. Steps 1-3 prove a sentence is in
the article it cites; they cannot notice that the article is about arithmetic. Wikipedia
serves the article on the number two at "2" and a list of British seaplanes at "AD 1",
and an early version of this build published both as year pages. A page is now rejected
unless its source article is the year's own article, or the decade, century or
millennium article the year falls inside.

What verification cannot do is read. It proves a sentence is in the article it cites; it
cannot notice that the sentence is a fragment, still carries wiki markup, or has lost its
subject. Those come out of a sweep over the rendered pages instead — looking for `[[`,
`{{`, `|` and `__NOTOC__`, for bullets that open on punctuation or on a back-reference to
a sentence that is not on the page, for duplicate bullets, for bullets with no source
link, and for leads that are not verbatim in their own source article. That sweep is what
found the six broken bullets in the first expansion and the eighteen montage-caption
leads later; run it after any change to `extract.py` or `build.py`.

A language model is used at exactly one point in the pipeline and it never touches
wording: `classify.py` embeds each sentence to decide which section of the page it
belongs under. That is the only decision on this site that no source makes, and for a
long time it was defended with the line that a mis-sorted bullet "looks untidy; it cannot
make the page say anything untrue". A heading is a claim about the row underneath it:
round thirteen found 910 accessions published as deaths, and round fifteen measured the
themed sections against the "Events > By topic > Religion / Literature / Astronomy"
headings the year articles carry and found we disagreed with the editors on 24% of the
rows that sit under one. `theming.py` is the gate for it and `classify.py` now reads that
heading where the article states one.

## Pipeline

Run in order from this directory. Steps are resumable and cache aggressively.

The order below is the incremental one, run on a site whose other page kinds already exist.
From a cold start — a fresh clone and `scripts/bootstrap.sh` — `dates.py`, `cross.py` and
`entities.py` have to run straight after `build.py`, because `coverage.py`, `famous.py` and
`surface.py` check those page kinds and cannot pass on pages that were never generated.
`scripts/reproduce.sh` uses that cold-start order.

```
python3      pipeline/harvest.py           # 5,022 year articles + revision ids -> data/raw
python3      pipeline/extract.py           # wikitext -> 162k provenanced claims -> data/claims
python3      pipeline/deaths.py            # + 215k deaths from "Deaths in <year>" (1977-2025)
python3      pipeline/notability.py        # article byte length per linked entity
python3      pipeline/categories.py        # Wikipedia's categories for 80,847 linked entities
python3  pipeline/classify.py          # section per claim (GPU; cached in data/sims.npz)
python3      pipeline/build.py --per-section 24 --births-deaths 14 --min-per-section 1
python3      pipeline/verify.py --live     # the guarantee above
python3      pipeline/coverage.py          # are the famous events actually there?
python3      pipeline/famous.py            # the wide version: 333 cases across regions
python3      pipeline/surface.py           # every OTHER page kind: shape, trace, fill
python3      pipeline/lifedates.py         # births/deaths vs Wikidata P569/P570
python3      pipeline/theming.py           # sections vs the article's own By-topic heading
python3      pipeline/machine.py --live    # /api, /dump, /api/search, /mcp vs the pages
python3      pipeline/dates.py             # 366 calendar pages -> data/site/dates
python3      pipeline/cross.py             # topic, decade and century cross-cuts
python3      pipeline/entities.py          # subject timelines
cd site && npx next build                           # 3,278 static pages -> site/out
python3      pipeline/postbuild.py         # not-found page, /api, sitemap, robots
#   ... deploy ...
python3      pipeline/indexnow.py          # tell Bing/Yandex/Seznam/Naver
```

`coverage.py` is the other half of the guarantee and exists because `verify.py` cannot
find a missing row: it proves that what we publish is correctly cited, and passes just as
happily on a 1969 page with no Moon landing on it. `coverage.py` holds a fixed list of
events any person would name as the defining event of its year, checks each one is really
in the built pages, and exits non-zero if one is gone. Every case marked `(regression)` in
it was genuinely missing at some point — see `COVERAGE-AUDIT.md` for how, and for the
seams we still do not harvest. Grow the list whenever a gap is found.

`surface.py` is the third of these and covers what the other two structurally cannot. Both
of them ask "is this famous thing on its year page?", and between them they look at two of
the seven page kinds this site publishes. `surface.py` names the other five (topic hubs,
decades, centuries, entity timelines, and the calendar pages as re-filings), checks the
SHAPE of every published sentence on all of them, checks that every cross-cut row traces
back to a row on its own year page with the same revision id, and checks the file count
against the Cloudflare 20,000 ceiling. It exists because `verify.py` cannot see a sentence
whose subject was deleted by the cleaner: verification re-derives the published text with
the same cleaner, so a deletion reproduces identically and matches. See COVERAGE-AUDIT.md,
round three.

`entities.py` resolves every link title through Wikipedia's redirect graph before grouping,
because a link target is a string and one subject is written many ways: grouping on the
string split 136 subjects across 284 pages (Persia and Iran, Sassanid and Sasanian Empire,
USSR and Soviet Union) and left 383 pages with no Wikidata id, since a redirect title has
no item of its own. Merged spellings are listed on the page, written to
`data/entities-merged.json` and 301'd by `postbuild.py`, so a merge never costs a URL.
`surface.py`'s **IDENTITY** check is the gate: no two published timelines may resolve to the
same article. See `SELF-REVIEW-2026-09-08-round14.md`. Note that `data/site/` is enumerated
as "one JSON per year" by four stages — never write anything else into it.

`lifedates.py` is the only gate that consults something outside Wikipedia. Every other
check asks whether we reproduced the source faithfully; this one asks whether the row is on
the right page at all, by comparing every Births/Deaths row that links its subject against
that person's date of birth (P569) and death (P570) in Wikidata. It found 910 rows filed
under Deaths that are not deaths -- "Truman becomes the 33rd president upon the death of
Franklin D. Roosevelt" was published as a death of 1945 -- see `SELF-REVIEW-2026-09-08-round13.md`.
Two things about it are load-bearing. Wikidata **stores** timestamps in the proleptic
Gregorian calendar and says separately which calendar the date was given in, so a Julian
date has to be converted back before it can be compared with what Wikipedia prints, or the
drift of the century reads as thousands of errors. And the gate is a **ratchet, not a
zero**: Wikipedia and Wikidata genuinely disagree about badly attested lives and about Old
Style dates, so `lifedates.baseline.json` holds the accepted counts and the gate fails only
a build that raises them. Re-accept with `--accept` when a change is deliberate.

`theming.py` is the fourth of these and it tests the only thing the other three cannot:
the section heading, which is not copied from anywhere. Wikipedia's year articles file
part of their Events section under headings the editors chose ("By topic > Religion",
"> Literature", "> Astronomy"); 1,921 of our claims sit under one whose reading is
unambiguous, `classify.py` had never read them, and on those rows the classifier
disagreed with the editors 464 times. Hand-checking put roughly 270 of those down as real
misfilings -- Sophocles' *Electra* under Crime & Justice, the Sienese bankers of the Gran
Tavola under Religion & Belief. `classify.py` now uses the heading where the article
states one, so those rows are sourced rather than guessed, and `--pure` re-derives every
theme while ignoring the heading so the gate stays a held-out test of the guessing part.
The second instrument in that file, a vote over the Wikipedia categories of the entities
each row links, is reported and deliberately NOT gated: it was built first, it claimed
three times as many defects, and hand-checking showed it was mostly measuring itself --
an entity's categories describe its whole existence, not what it is doing in one
sentence. See `SELF-REVIEW-2026-09-08-round15.md`.

`machine.py` is the fifth, and it exists because every other gate in this list ends at the
rendered HTML while the product does not. `/api`, `/dump`, `/api/search`, `/mcp` and
`llms.txt` are what an agent actually consumes, and until round sixteen no test read a byte
of them. It mirrors every `/api` file against the payload its page was rendered from,
round-trips the three NDJSON dumps, resolves every posting in the full-text index, checks
every URL the docs promise, and with `--live` calls the search endpoint and every MCP tool
for real and cross-checks the rows they return. Its SEARCHQ block is `coverage.py` pointed
at search: twelve queries anyone would type and the answer that has to come back on top.
That is what caught `/api/search?q=Apollo+11` answering with a 1920 newspaper item about
Goddard — every row it returned was real, published and correctly cited, and the order was
nonsense. See `SELF-REVIEW-2026-09-08-round16.md`.

`famous.py` is `coverage.py` widened from 51 cases to 333 and spread deliberately across
regions and eras. `coverage.py` stays small so it can gate every build; `famous.py` is the
sweep to run after any change to extraction, ranking or filing. Both are in
`COVERAGE-AUDIT.md`.

A second model (`a second, unrelated frontier model`, via an outside-auditor script) was run over the pipeline
on 2026-09-08 as an independent reviewer. Treat anything it says as a hypothesis and
measure it before acting: 13 of its 60 claims were real, and its largest finding was
false. The verdict table is `LUNA-REVIEW-2026-09-08.md`.

`dates.py` must run **before** `next build` (the build reads `data/site/dates/`) and
`postbuild.py` **after** every build, or the not-found page silently dies and the sitemap
and `/api` go stale. `indexnow.py` runs **after** the deploy, so every address it submits
is already live.

`classify.py` caches per claim id, so growing the site embeds only the claims it has
never seen — adding 1,800 year pages cost about two GPU minutes, not a re-run.

`deaths.py` runs between them and must: it merges into the same claims files, and its
claims carry their own `source`, because from 1977 Wikipedia keeps a year's deaths in a
separate article ("Deaths in October 2011") and a bullet has to cite the revision it really
came from. `build.py` reads that per-claim source for the citation and lists the extra
articles in the page footer; `verify.py` looks for the line in that revision and
re-downloads it live like any other.

**`classify.py` writes its answer back into `data/claims`, and `extract.py` rewrites those
files.** So any run of `extract.py` erases every theme, and the next `build.py` dies with
`KeyError: 'theme'` after it has already emptied `data/site`. Extract and classify are one
step, always run in that order, never one without the other.

Every script takes `--help`.

### Notes on the parts that were not obvious

**Nested bullets.** Where several things happened on one date, Wikipedia writes a bare
date bullet with the events nested underneath. An extractor that only reads top-level
bullets silently drops them — that is how the first run of this pipeline produced a 1969
page with no Moon landing on it. Nested lines are now read and inherit the parent's
date, while `raw` still holds the untouched source line so verification stays exact.

**Ranking.** A year like 1969 yields 325 usable entries and the page shows about 45, so
the ranking decides what the site is actually about. Ranking by how many year articles
link an entity rewards "Spain" and "France"; ranking by raw article size rewards them
too, since they are enormous articles. What works is article size discounted by how
generic the entity is — a big article about something specific to this year. That is
what puts Columbus at the top of 1492 and the Moon landing in 1969.

**Leads.** The muted line under the year heading is not a summary we wrote. Most year
articles carry an editor-written montage caption listing the year's headline events;
where it exists it is used verbatim, and where it does not the three highest-ranked
entries stand in. A summary in our own words would be the one claim on the page with
nothing behind it.

**The 404 collision.** Next writes the not-found page to `out/404.html` — and it writes
the year page for 404 CE to `out/404.html` as well. The year page wins, so for a while
the live site answered every unpublished year and every typo URL with the year 404 CE
page (the martyrdom of Saint Telemachus) rather than "no sourced records for this year".
`postbuild.py` moves the year page to `out/404/index.html`, writes the real not-found
page over `404.html`, and adds a `_redirects` line so /404 still reaches the year. Both
halves of that were measured against a preview deployment rather than assumed, because
Cloudflare prefers a bare file to a directory index and would otherwise have hidden the
year page instead.

**Montage captions carry a reading order.** A year article's image footer often opens
"From left to right, top to bottom:" — an instruction about a picture grid that is not
on our page. Eighteen leads shipped with that prefix before the strip was widened beyond
"From top to bottom:".

**Disambiguation pages.** Bare numeric titles like `1000` and `911` are disambiguation
pages on Wikipedia — the year article lives at `AD 1000`. Harvesting the bare title
gives you an article about a pickup truck. `harvest.py` detects and skips these.

## Coverage

The rebuild covers **2,909 published years**, up from the 809 the old site had.

- **Every year from 1 CE to 2025 CE, with no gaps at all.** The old site had 795 of them
  and skipped, for instance, everything between 337 and 378 CE.
- 884 BCE years: unbroken from 1 BCE back to 516 BCE, then 368 further years scattered
  back to 3000 BCE wherever a source actually says something about that year.
- Years with nothing sourced to them are left out rather than published as an empty page.
  Asking for one — /2026, or 1274 BCE at /-1273 — returns a real 404 that names the year
  and links the nearest year that does have records.
- `sitemap.xml` lists all 3,078 pages plus the index, and `robots.txt` points at it, so
  discovery does not depend on crawling one 1 MB directory page.

Pages carry more of what the source has, too: up to 24 entries per themed section and 14
each for births and deaths, against 10 and 8 before. 1969 now shows 172 of its 619
sourced entries where the old site showed 80.

### Years without their own article

Wikipedia does not give every year an article. AD 4 redirects to "0s", 2900 BC lives
inside "29th century BC", and 1602 BC inside "1600s BC (decade)". Those are good sources
covering ten or a hundred years at once, so publishing one wholesale under a single year
puts entries on a page that are not about that year: the first build of this expansion
had Ramesses II's whole reign on the 1274 BCE page and a 771 BC birth on the 776 BCE
page.

So when the source is not the year's own article, an entry is kept only where the article
itself attributes it to this year — the section it sits under is that year's heading
("=== AD 44 ==="), or the line opens by naming it ("c. 3000 BC: Camels are domesticated
in Egypt"). A line reading "c. 3300 BC – 2600 BC: ..." names a span, not a year, and is
dropped, as is "Before 3000 BC: ...". The lead is dropped too, because a decade
article's summary summarises the decade. A year left with nothing simply gets no page.

## Site

`site/` is a Next.js app that renders the payloads in `data/site/`, statically exported.
The markup and Tailwind classes were taken from the live site so the two are
indistinguishable, with three deliberate differences:

- every bullet ends in a small `source` link to the cited revision and section anchor;
- each page has a footer stating the article, revision id and retrieval date, and the
  CC BY-SA 4.0 licence the text is reused under;
- the directory says the entries are quoted from cited revisions.

There are three page kinds. `/<year>` renders one year. `/on/<month>-<day>` renders one
calendar day across every year, built by `dates.py`, which re-files the same sentences and
the same citations under the day they name -- 366 pages, 54,415 entries, and the only part
of the site that is not a near-duplicate of a Wikipedia article. `/api` documents the
static JSON copies of both. Every date on a year page links to that day and every year on a
date page links back, which is what makes the site one connected graph rather than 3,275
orphans. What all of that is for, and what is still missing, is in `SEO.md`.

Sections are per-era rather than fixed, as on the live site. The taxonomy is
Geopolitics & Diplomacy, Conflict & Security, Exploration & Expansion, Religion &
Belief, Economy & Finance, Technology & Infrastructure, Science & Discovery, Health &
Medicine, Crime & Justice, Disasters & Accidents, Climate & Environment, Culture &
Society, then Births and Deaths. A section only appears when the year has entries for
it.

## Deployment

The rebuild **is** slashyear.com. It runs on Cloudflare Pages (project
`slashyear-rebuild`, also reachable at https://slashyear-rebuild.pages.dev). The apex and
`www` are proxied CNAMEs to `slashyear-rebuild.pages.dev` in the slashyear.com zone, and
both are registered as custom domains on the Pages project with an active certificate.
The old Vercel deployment is no longer referenced by DNS.

To redeploy:

```
cd site && npx next build && cd .. && python3 pipeline/postbuild.py
cd site && CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN=... CLOUDFLARE_ACCOUNT_ID=... \
  npx wrangler pages deploy out --project-name slashyear-rebuild --branch main
```

`--branch main` is production. Any other branch name deploys a preview at
`https://<branch>.slashyear-rebuild.pages.dev`, which is the way to test anything about
how Cloudflare resolves a URL before it reaches the live domain.

Credentials are in `.secrets/cloudflare.env (gitignored, or the CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN environment variable)`.

Two redirects, in two different places, and it matters which:

- **`/404` -> `/404/`** is a line in `_redirects`, written by `postbuild.py`.
- **`www.slashyear.com` -> `slashyear.com`** is a *zone* rule, in the Cloudflare dynamic
  redirect ruleset for slashyear.com, created 2026-09-07. It cannot live in `_redirects`:
  Cloudflare Pages accepts a host-qualified line there and then ignores it -- measured
  live, www kept answering 200 with the rule deployed. Until the zone rule existed, every
  page had two working addresses.

## Pipeline order (updated 2026-09-07)

`verify.py` → `dates.py` → `cross.py` → `next build` (from inside `site/`) → `postbuild.py`
→ `audit.py` → deploy → `indexnow.py`.

`montage.py` (round twelve) is the coverage test that writes its own cases: the montage
caption on every year article from 1900 on is the editors' own list of what mattered, and
a caption event that we harvested and then dropped to fit a section cap is a hole no case
list of mine would have named (1940 had lost Dunkirk and Katyn, 1961 the Bay of Pigs).
`build.montage_picks()` pins those rows into their section on top of the cap.

`integrity.py` (round eleven) runs alongside `surface.py` and is the only test that reads
the wikitext back with a **different** renderer than the one that produced the site —
`verify.py` re-renders with `clean_line()`, the same function it is testing, so it agrees
with itself by construction and cannot fail on a cleaner bug. It also proves the page
metadata describes its own rows (counts, spans, entity headers) and that every internal
link has a page.

`reclean.py` is how a cleaner fix ships **without** a re-extract: it re-renders only the
claims whose `raw` matches a regex, rebuilding id/date/body/links exactly as `extract()`
would while leaving `theme` and the ranking fields alone. Use it whenever `extract.py`
changes but the harvested wikitext has not — a full `extract.py` run erases every theme.

- `cross.py` writes `data/site/cross/` (decades, centuries, subjects) and must run before
  the build, like `dates.py`.
- `next build` MUST be run with `site/` as the working directory. The data loaders resolve
  `../data/site` from `process.cwd()`, so `next build site` from the repo root silently
  reads nothing and the build dies in `generateStaticParams`.
- `postbuild.py` also deletes Next's React payload files. There are four per page and
  Cloudflare Pages rejects any deployment over 20,000 files; the site ships plain `<a>`
  links so nothing needs them.
- `audit.py` reads the exported site back and exits non-zero on a defect. Nothing deploys
  without it being clean.

## Round three additions (2026-09-07 evening) — the machine-readable half

Pipeline order is now:

`verify.py` → `dates.py` → `cross.py` → **`entities.py`** → `next build` (from inside
`site/`) → `postbuild.py` → `audit.py` → deploy → `indexnow.py`.

**`pipeline/entities.py`** is the fourth cross-cut. `dates.py` re-files the corpus by
calendar day and `cross.py` by decade, century and subject; this one re-files it by the
entity a sentence is *about*, using the `[[wikilinks]]` in the source line. 2,941 subjects
clear the floor of eight distinct years and get a page at `/timeline/<slug>`. Each is
resolved against Wikidata to a QID (2,722 of them have one), which is what makes the JSON
joinable with any other dataset rather than just readable.

Two traps paid for here:

- **`wbgetentities` rejects `normalize=1` for a multi-title request** ("only allowed if
  exactly one site and one page have been given") and fails the *whole batch*. The first
  run returned 0 Wikidata ids for 2,941 entities and reported success.
- **Map the answer back through each entity's own `sitelinks.enwiki.title`, never by
  position.** The API keys its response by QID and silently drops titles it cannot resolve,
  so a positional zip mislabels every entity after the first miss.

**`postbuild.py`** grew five writers:

- `write_entities_api` — `/api/entity/<slug>.json` + `/api/entities.json`.
- `write_search_index` — one 328 KB file of every subject, held in memory by the search
  endpoint.
- `write_text_index` — a real inverted index over all 86,902 sentences: postings bucketed
  by first letter (36 files), sentences in 256-row shards (335 files), plus each shard's
  year span so a year-filtered query can discard whole shards before fetching anything.
- `write_dumps` — `/dump/{events,years,entities}.ndjson.gz` and a Frictionless
  `datapackage.json`.
- `write_llms` — `/llms.txt` and `/llms-full.txt`.

⚠️ **`strip_rsc` eats any `.txt` file it does not recognise.** It exists to delete Next's
React payloads, which are `.txt`, and it deleted `llms.txt` the first time it was written.
Anything new at the site root with a `.txt` extension must be added to its `keep` set.

**Cloudflare Pages Functions** now live in `site/functions/`, with the code they share in
`site/edge/` (outside `functions/`, so it is bundled as a module and never routed):

- `functions/api/search.js` → `/api/search?q=&from=&to=&limit=`.
- `functions/mcp.js` → `/mcp`, a stateless Model Context Protocol server over streamable
  HTTP with five tools. No sessions, no SSE, no storage; every request is one complete
  JSON-RPC exchange answered from static assets via `env.ASSETS`.

`wrangler pages deploy out` picks up `functions/` from the **current working directory**,
which is why the deploy has to be run from inside `site/` — the same reason `next build`
does.

Search ranking notes, both found by testing real queries rather than by reading the code:

- Substring matching on a short or numeric token is worse than useless. Half the subject
  labels carry a disambiguating date range, so `Apollo 11` scored a hit on
  `Jin dynasty (1115–1234)`, and `printing press` matched four empresses through "em-press".
  Tokens now only count at a word boundary and numbers only as whole words.
- A bag of words cannot tell you the words were adjacent, so `black death` ranked
  "Clearchus, tyrant of Heraclea on the Black Sea, is murdered" above the Black Death. The
  phrase check happens after hydration, on the sentences already fetched.

**`audit.py`** now also checks the 2,941 timeline pages sentence by sentence against their
source payloads, the same as year, date and subject pages. 6,832 pages, 262,709 bullets,
317,965 links, clean.

### The Cloudflare setting that was undoing all of it

`is_robots_txt_managed` was `true` on the zone, which makes Cloudflare prepend its own
block to `robots.txt` — `Content-Signal: ai-train=no` plus `Disallow: /` for GPTBot,
ClaudeBot, CCBot, Google-Extended, Amazonbot, Bytespider, Applebot-Extended and
meta-externalagent — ahead of ours. The file in this repository said `Allow: /` the whole
time. Fixed with:

```
PUT /zones/<zone>/bot_management
{"is_robots_txt_managed": false, "ai_bots_protection": "disabled"}
POST /zones/<zone>/purge_cache  {"files": ["https://slashyear.com/robots.txt"]}
```

Check `robots.txt` on the live host after any zone change; the build's copy proves nothing.
